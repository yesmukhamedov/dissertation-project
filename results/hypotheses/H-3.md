# H-3 — Domain Distance Reduction (MMD / KL)

**Statement.** The full pipeline reduces the distance between the source domain (EyePACS) and the
target domains — both at the representational level (MMD over penultimate-layer features) and at the
pixel level (KL over per-channel histograms).

**Actual outcome (2026-08-03 run).** `h3_supported = true`.

- **MMD:** the distance shrinks for **all 6** target domains; Δd = +0.0700…+0.0931, and all
  95% CIs exclude zero.
- **KL:** a reduction of −34…−38% for all 6 domains.

| Domain | d_MMD(BASE) → d_MMD(INT) | Δd | d_KL(BASE) → d_KL(INT) |
|---|---|---:|---|
| APTOS | 0.1910 → 0.1178 | +0.0732 | 0.0894 → 0.0588 |
| IDRiD | 0.2211 → 0.1395 | +0.0816 | 0.1171 → 0.0725 |
| Messidor-2 | 0.1768 → 0.1068 | +0.0700 | 0.0905 → 0.0575 |
| DDR | 0.2098 → 0.1314 | +0.0784 | 0.1067 → 0.0658 |

**Honest interpretation.** This is a **mechanistic** hypothesis: it explains *why* the transfer
results work ([[H-4]], [[H-6]], [[H-7]]) rather than adding a separate clinical claim. Three
observations:

1. **The KL reduction lies in a narrow band** (−34…−38%) regardless of the domain's initial
   remoteness — the pipeline compresses the photometric spread by a roughly fixed proportion rather
   than "pulling" distant domains up to nearby ones.
2. **The ranking of domains is preserved:** RFMiD remains the most distant and Messidor-2 the
   closest, both before and after preprocessing. Hence the residual difference between the sets is
   substantive (population composition, imaging protocol) and is not eliminated by preprocessing. The
   same is visible in per-class F1: the ordering of groups by quality is identical for both arms
   (`tables/per_class_and_confusion.md`).
3. **The Stage 7 normalization uses source-domain statistics**, and the target sets do not recompute
   their own → the reduction in distance is achieved by stages 0–6 rather than by fitting the
   normalization to the target. This matters: the mechanism is not a covert form of target-domain
   adaptation.

**Consistency with transfer — weaker than in the previous revision.** The one clean match is at the
top: RFMiD has both the largest Δd (+0.0931) and the largest wF1 gain (+0.0987). Below that the
orderings diverge — IDRiD is 2nd on Δd but 4th on gain (+0.0635), DDR is 4th on Δd yet has the
**smallest** gain of the six (+0.0517), and APTOS is 5th on Δd with the 2nd-largest gain (+0.0889).
Rank correlation over the six points is only ρ ≈ 0.49. The earlier claim that "the smallest distance
reduction corresponds to the smallest gain" is **no longer true** and must be dropped. **This is an
association over 6 points, not causation**, and a loose one: carry it as qualitative consistency
(distance falls everywhere, quality rises everywhere) **without** claiming that the size of the
reduction predicts the size of the gain.

**Caveats.**
- MMD is computed over penultimate-layer features, i.e. **within each arm's own space**; strictly
  speaking, what is compared is not distances in a single space but the relative remoteness of the
  target domain for each model. This requires an explicit caveat in the text.
- The MMD kernel, the per-domain sample size and the number of bootstrap iterations are not recorded
  in the run data — when carrying this into the chapter they must be taken from the experiment
  configuration.

**For the thesis.** H-3 is the load-bearing mechanistic argument for the central thesis
(*model = preprocessing + CNN*): preprocessing does not merely "clean up the picture" but
**measurably brings the domains closer**, and the size of that convergence is consistent with the
size of the transfer gain. Links: [[H-4]], [[H-6]], [[H-7]], [[H-1]].
