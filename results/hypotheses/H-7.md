# H-7 — Clinical Degradation Resistance (Exp 5)

**Statement.** The full pipeline degrades LESS than baseline on external clinical sets (IDRiD,
Messidor-2): Δ_drop_full < Δ_drop_baseline, where Δ_drop = wF1_in-domain − wF1_external.

**Actual outcome (2026-08-03 run 2).** `h7_supported` — **NOT SUPPORTED as written (0 of 2 sets)**.
The only hypothesis of the run that is not confirmed. Two reasonable readings give different
answers, and it is precisely this analysis that turns it into a methodological result rather than a
misfire: in absolute performance the pipeline wins significantly on both sets, and the failure is
explained by a structural bias in the Δ_drop metric itself.

> ⚠️ **Change from the previous revision**, where H-7 was reported as **partial (1 of 2)**: IDRiD then
> passed by −0.0045. It now flips to +0.0020, so the criterion fails on both sets. Report **0/2**.

## (a) The hypothesis as written — sign reversed on both sets

| Set | Δ_drop (C) | Δ_drop (D) | Δ_drop(D) − Δ_drop(C) | Δ_full < Δ_base? |
|-------|-----------:|-----------:|----------------------:|:----------------:|
| IDRiD | **0.1581** | 0.1601 | +0.0020 | ✗ |
| Messidor-2 | **0.1255** | 0.1384 | +0.0129 | ✗ |

Neither gap is large — the IDRiD one (+0.0020) is far inside the width of the confidence intervals on
the absolute metrics — but both point the wrong way. In the form "the pipeline is more resistant to
clinical degradation" the hypothesis is **not supported** by these data; what is supported is the
more precise formulation — section (b) and "Final interpretation" below.

## (b) Absolute performance on the external sets — confirmed on both, significantly

| Set | n | wF1 (C) | wF1 (D) | Δ | 95% CI (Δ) | p (1-sided) |
|-------|--:|--------:|--------:|--:|------------|------------:|
| IDRiD | 413 | 0.5957 | 0.6592 | +0.0635 | [+0.0445, +0.0919] | 0.0021 |
| Messidor-2 | 1 744 | 0.6283 | 0.6809 | +0.0526 | [+0.0264, +0.0716] | 0.0138 |

## Why the two formulations diverge

Δ_drop is measured from each arm's **own** in-domain level, and the pipeline's is 6.55 pp higher
(0.8193 against 0.7538). An arm with a higher starting point is bound to lose more in absolute units
to arrive at the same external level — that is, **the Δ_drop metric structurally penalizes the
stronger arm**. Relative degradation shows how little is at stake:

| Set | relative drop (C) | relative drop (D) | favours |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| IDRiD | 21.0% | **19.5%** | pipeline |
| Messidor-2 | **16.6%** | 16.9% | baseline (marginally) |

Proportionally the arms degrade almost identically, one set leaning each way by a fraction of a
percentage point. There is no resistance effect in either direction; the pipeline simply starts
higher and finishes higher.

## Final interpretation

The pipeline makes the model **better at every point** — in-domain and at both external clinics —
without changing the relative size of the drop on transfer. This claim is narrower than H-7 and
replaces it: it is confirmed significantly on both sets and is of greater practical value: **on
deployment at a new clinic the expected quality is higher** (+0.064 on IDRiD, +0.053 on Messidor-2),
and it is this quantity that determines clinical usability, whereas Δ_drop is a quantity derived from
each arm's own in-domain level.

Consistency with [[H-3]] is limited to direction: Messidor-2 has the smallest MMD reduction of the
six domains (+0.0700) and also the smaller of these two wF1 gains — but across all six domains the
size of the distance reduction does not predict the size of the gain, so this is not evidence of a
quantitative link.

## Caveats

- Evaluation uses **fold 0** checkpoints; there is no between-fold variance.
- Δ_drop as a resistance metric is poorly defined when the in-domain levels are unequal (see above);
  relative degradation is the fairer quantity, but it is not separately recorded in the run data.
- The same two sets appear in exp6 as camera groups (`kowa_idrid`, `topcon_messidor2`) — the numbers
  coincide ([[H-6]]).

## For the thesis

Report as **not supported in its original wording**, with an explicit analysis of the metric: "the
integrated configuration does not reduce the size of the drop on transfer to external clinical sets —
on the Δ_drop criterion it is marginally worse on both sets — but delivers statistically
significantly higher absolute performance on both (+0.064 and +0.053)".

The methodological observation carries the weight here and should be stated separately as a
contribution for §5.4: **Δ_drop is not a neutral measure of resistance when the baseline levels are
unequal** — it penalizes the stronger arm by construction, which is exactly what these two sets
demonstrate. A negative result on the letter of the hypothesis that identifies a defect in the
criterion is a legitimate finding, and stronger than a borderline pass would have been; it should be
presented that way rather than minimized.

Contrast with [[exp7-and-SSL]]: when training **within** the target clinical domain the pipeline
delivers +0.080 wF1.
