# H-7 — Clinical Degradation Resistance (Exp 5)

**Statement.** The full pipeline degrades LESS than baseline on external clinical sets (IDRiD,
Messidor-2): Δ_drop_full < Δ_drop_baseline, where Δ_drop = wF1_in-domain − wF1_external.

**Actual outcome (2026-08-02 run).** `h7_supported` — **PARTIAL (1 of 2 sets)**.
The only hypothesis of the run that is not fully confirmed. Two reasonable readings give different
answers, and it is precisely this analysis that turns it into a methodological result rather than a
misfire: in absolute performance the pipeline wins significantly on both sets, and the discrepancy is
explained by a bias in the Δ_drop metric itself.

## (a) The hypothesis as written — confirmed on IDRiD, sign reversed on Messidor-2

| Set | Δ_drop (C) | Δ_drop (D) | Δ_drop(D) − Δ_drop(C) | Δ_full < Δ_base? |
|-------|-----------:|-----------:|----------------------:|:----------------:|
| IDRiD | 0.1618 | 0.1573 | −0.0045 | ✓ (margin within noise) |
| Messidor-2 | 0.1268 | 0.1413 | +0.0145 | ✗ |

On IDRiD the pipeline's advantage is 0.0045 — an order of magnitude smaller than the width of the
confidence intervals on the absolute metrics. On Messidor-2 the sign is **reversed**. Therefore, in
the form "the pipeline is more resistant to clinical degradation" the hypothesis is not supported by
these data; what is supported is the more precise formulation — section (b) and "Final
interpretation" below.

## (b) Absolute performance on the external sets — confirmed on both, significantly

| Set | n | wF1 (C) | wF1 (D) | Δ | 95% CI (Δ) | p (1-sided) |
|-------|--:|--------:|--------:|--:|------------|------------:|
| IDRiD | 413 | 0.5920 | 0.6620 | +0.0700 | [+0.0463, +0.0937] | 0.0021 |
| Messidor-2 | 1 744 | 0.6270 | 0.6780 | +0.0510 | [+0.0284, +0.0736] | 0.0138 |

## Why the two formulations diverge

Δ_drop is measured from each arm's **own** in-domain level, and the pipeline's is 6.55 pp higher
(0.8193 against 0.7538). An arm with a higher starting point is bound to lose more in absolute units
to arrive at the same external level — that is, **the Δ_drop metric systematically penalizes the
stronger arm**. Relative degradation confirms this: on Messidor-2 baseline loses 16.8% of its level
and the pipeline 17.2%, i.e. **proportionally the arms degrade practically identically**, and the
pipeline simply starts higher and finishes higher.

## Final interpretation

The pipeline makes the model **better at every point** — in-domain and at both external clinics —
without changing the relative size of the drop on transfer. This claim is narrower and more precise
than H-7, but it is confirmed significantly on both sets and is of greater practical value: **on
deployment at a new clinic the expected quality is higher** (+0.070 on IDRiD, +0.051 on Messidor-2),
and it is this quantity that determines clinical usability, whereas Δ_drop is a quantity derived from
each arm's own in-domain level.

This is consistent with [[H-3]]: the smallest MMD-distance reduction is precisely for Messidor-2
(+0.0630, the lowest of the six domains), and the same set has the smallest wF1 gain (+0.051) and is
the only case where the pipeline's Δ_drop is worse.

## Caveats

- Evaluation uses **fold 0** checkpoints; there is no between-fold variance.
- Δ_drop as a resistance metric is poorly defined when the in-domain levels are unequal (see above);
  relative degradation is the fairer quantity, but it is not separately recorded in the run data.
- The same two sets appear in exp6 as camera groups (`kowa_idrid`, `topcon_messidor2`) — the numbers
  coincide ([[H-6]]).
