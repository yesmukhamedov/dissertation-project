# Presentation and Talk — norms, slide structure, speech script, Q&A

> Reconstructed from real samples of this council:

> Regulatory basis — **§7.7 of the Council Statute** (session procedure, where the candidate is given the floor).

## Norms (across 12 samples)

Based on the analysis of real defenses, the following median and normative indicators have been established:
*   **Time limit:** the normative limit for the presentation is **20 minutes**. This is a strict requirement frequently announced by the chairman ("The deadline is 20 minutes", "Перед вами 20 минут").
*   **Number of slides:** median **37**, ranging from 17 to 51 slides. Given the 20-minute limit, the optimal volume is around 25–35 slides.
*   **Talk duration:** median **17.7 minutes** (from 12 valid defenses), confirming the effort to stay within the 20-minute limit.
*   **Speech pace:** median **90 words per minute (WPM)**.
*   **Text density:** median **114 words per slide**. This is considered an anti-pattern (overloaded slides). It is recommended to reduce density to 70-80 words, moving the rest to the oral speech.
*   **Questions (Q&A):** an average of **27 questions** are asked (ranging from 2 to 57).

## Canonical Slide Order and Timing (Skeleton)

The presentation is built in a strict order; deviations (e.g., omitting the "Conclusion" or "Publications" sections) are structural violations.

| # | Slide Section | Typical Headings | Time Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title Slide** | Topic, Name, Supervisors, University logo | 0.5 min |
| 2 | **Relevance** | Relevance, Актуальность | 2-3 min |
| 3 | **Goal and Objectives** | Goal / Objectives (often on one slide) | 1 min |
| 4 | **Object and Subject** | Object and subject of research | 0.5 min |
| 5 | **Novelty / Provisions** | Scientific Novelty, Provisions to be Defended (numbered list) | 2 min |
| 6 | **Architecture / Methods** | Architecture, Methods, Proposed approach (more diagrams, less text) | 3-4 min |
| 7 | **Experiments and Results** | Results, Experimental evaluation, Metrics (charts, tables) | 5-6 min |
| 8 | **Implementation** | Practical significance, Implementation acts | 1 min |
| 9 | **Conclusion** | Conclusion, Выводы (text list) | 1 min |
| 10 | **Approbation / Publications** | Publications (list with journal screenshots) | 1 min |

**Formatting (Visual Design):**
The standard university template is used. Text should not dominate graphics in the "Architecture" and "Results" sections.

## Talk Skeleton (Speech Script)

It is recommended not to read slides word-for-word, but to elaborate on the bullet points. The main language of the talk is chosen in advance, but the presentation slides can be bilingual.

### Opening Formulas
*   **EN:** "Dear Chairman, dear members of the dissertation council! Let me present to your attention the dissertation work on the topic <...>."
*   **RU:** "Уважаемый председатель, уважаемые члены диссертационного совета! Разрешите представить вашему вниманию диссертационную работу на тему <...>."
*   **KZ:** "Құрметті төраға, құрметті диссертациялық кеңес мүшелері! Сіздердің назарларыңызға <...> тақырыбындағы диссертациялық жұмысты ұсынуға рұқсат етіңіздер."

### Transitions
*   **EN:** "Slide <N> shows the proposed architecture...", "Moving on to the experimental results..."
*   **RU:** "На слайде <N> представлена архитектура...", "Переходя к результатам экспериментов..."

### Closing Formulas
*   **EN:** "This concludes my presentation. Thank you for your attention! I will be happy to answer your questions."
*   **RU:** "Доклад окончен. Спасибо за внимание! Буду рад(а) ответить на ваши вопросы."
*   **KZ:** "Баяндама аяқталды. Назарларыңызға рақмет! Сұрақтарыңызға жауап беруге дайынмын."

*Reference on speaking order in the session:*
The chairman opens the session; the scientific secretary of the dissertation council presents the candidate's personal file, after which council members may ask the secretary questions about it. The chairman then gives the floor to the candidate for the presentation, after which the Q&A session begins (first questions from council members, then from all present). Then the supervisors and reviewers speak.

## Q&A Preparation

Typical questions revolve around methodology, metrics, implementation, and limitations. It is necessary to prepare answers for the following types of questions (examples from real defenses):

1.  **Methodology (comparison):** "What are the main advantages and limitations of your models compared to <...>?"
2.  **Limitations and conditions:** "What are the functioning conditions, operating conditions of your system, what are the limitations of your system?"
3.  **Data cleaning and volume:** "How did you identify and handle <...> in a data set exceeding <...> records?"
4.  **Significance/Implementation:** "Then what is the point of your work?", "So the teacher starts developing, then they use this <...> of yours, right?"
5.  **Feature combination:** "You have a lot of different features. How can you combine them?"
6.  **Quality metrics:** "Why are we using <...> metrics?"
7.  **Clarification of details (time/speed):** "Time passes between these <...>. How do you account for <...>?"

## Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Number of slides does not exceed 35.

- [ ] Slides are not overloaded with text (no more than 70-80 words per slide).
- [ ] Canonical order is followed (from Relevance to Publications).
- [ ] "Conclusion" and "Publications" sections are present.
- [ ] Brief answers are prepared for typical questions on limitations, data, and metrics.
