# Observed norms: what dissertations defended at this council actually look like

> **Purpose:** the Instruction sets *ceilings* (≤ 300 pp.) and a list of mandatory elements. It does
> not say how long a chapter is, how deep the numbering goes, or what the body is divided into. This
> file records what the council actually receives — measured across **all 16 dissertations** the IITU
> council has published (defences 2022-06 … 2026-06). Use it together with
> [[structure|the structural template]] and [[../02-formatting/gost-formatting|the GOST formatting
> rules]]: the template says what elements are mandatory, this file says what size, depth and content
> they have in practice.
>
> **Measured 2026-08-19** from the published PDFs — page counts and section boundaries (sections 1–3),
> the tables of contents and body headings of all 16 (sections 4–6), then, on the full text of the
> corpus (≈ 6.2 M characters, 7,045 paragraphs), the prose conventions (section 7), and finally the
> page geometry — indents, alignment, spacing, caption placement — from the PDF coordinates of every
> line, checked against rendered pages of three samples (section 8). Every volume figure was
> cross-checked against the "Structure and volume of the work" sentence in the candidate's own
> annotation, and the two agree wherever the annotation states a figure.
>
> Per the anonymity principle of this knowledge base, samples are keyed by **defence year +
> programme code**, never by name.

**Reading of the numbers.** Not one of the 16 approaches the 300-page ceiling; the largest is
**153 pp.**, and 14 of 16 are **≤ 135 pp.** The working norm at this council is a volume of
**≈ 90–130 pages including appendices**, of which **≈ 70–100 pages** are main text. A volume of
200 pages or more is not forbidden by the Instruction, but it has **no precedent in this council's
published record** and will read to reviewers as a work that was never edited down.

**The word count is the sharper measure**, because page counts move with how many figures a work
carries: the main text of these dissertations runs **15,000–31,000 words**, median ≈ 22,700. A
manuscript of 50,000 words cannot be squeezed into the page norm by formatting; it is twice the
corpus maximum whatever the layout.

Two samples state the convention explicitly in their own annotation — "the total length of the main
text is 98 pages, excluding appendices", "the total volume of the main text is 90 pages, not counting
appendices" — i.e. authors here quote the *main text* figure and declare appendices excluded, exactly
as [[../02-formatting/gost-formatting|the Length section]] allows.

## 2. Reported statistics (from the candidates' own annotations)

Of the samples whose annotation states them: illustrations **9–46**, tables **4–19**, sources in the
reference list **97–154**. Works with more than 20 tables do not occur in the corpus; the numeric
detail that would produce them is either folded into figures or moved to an appendix of supplementary
tables.

## 3. Front matter — what is actually present

- **Designations and abbreviations** — in effectively every sample. Treat as mandatory.
- **Definitions** — in about 9 of 16.
- **Normative references** — in about 6 of 16, i.e. **routinely omitted** in engineering works of
  this council, even though the Instruction lists the element.
- Front matter is short: median **5–6 pages** for title page + contents + all three lists combined.

## 4. The Introduction — 8 to 11 run-in rubrics, several of them merged

The Introduction is continuous prose with **bold run-in headings**, not numbered subsections, and it
does not appear in the contents below its own line. The rubrics observed, in their usual order:

1. **Relevance of the research** (often closing with a link to a state programme or a Presidential Address)
2. **Research aim and objectives** — aim and the numbered objectives under **one** heading
3. **Object and subject of research** — one heading, two sentences
4. **Theoretical and methodological framework**
5. **Scientific novelty**
6. **Provisions submitted for defence**
7. **Theoretical and practical significance** — **one** heading, not two
8. **Reliability of the results** (in a minority; the Instruction's "reliability" is usually carried
   inside the novelty/methodology prose)
9. **Approbation of results and publications** — **one** heading, not two
10. **Connection with state programmes** (in a minority)
11. **Structure and volume of the work** — the volume sentence plus a paragraph-per-chapter walkthrough

**Rubrics that do not occur anywhere in the corpus:** a named "Research hypothesis" item, a separate
"Empirical basis", and separately headed "Theoretical significance" / "Practical significance" or
"Approbation" / "Publications". The word *hypothesis* appears in these works only in its statistical
or diagnostic sense — never as a labelled system of research hypotheses carried through the body.

## 5. Chapter architecture — how the body is divided

**Chapter count: 3–7, median 4** (8 of 16 have exactly four). Distribution: three chapters ×3,
four ×8, five ×2, six ×2, seven ×1.

The recurring plan, in this order:

| Position | Role | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 | **Review / analysis of the state of the field, ending in the problem statement** | 15 of 16 |
| Middle chapters (1–3 of them) | **Methods, model, and the development of the proposed system** — theory is folded in here or in Chapter 1, never given a chapter of its own | all |
| Last chapter | **Experiments and results** (11 of 16) or **implementation of the delivered system** (5 of 16) | all |

Three consequences worth stating explicitly, because each is a place where a longer manuscript
naturally drifts out of the genre:

- **There is no standalone "theoretical foundations" chapter separate from a "methodology" chapter.**
  Where a sample's Chapter 1 is titled for theory, it *is* the review chapter and it ends with the
  problem formulation.
- **Comparative analysis against published work is a subsection of the experiments chapter**
  ("Comparative analysis with existing solutions", "Comparative analysis of the algorithms",
  "Benchmarking"), never a chapter. **No sample has a validation or reliability chapter**; statistical
  validation lives inside the results subsections.
- **A system/software chapter, where present, describes what was built** — architecture, interface,
  deployment, screenshots — rather than specifying requirements. It usually precedes the experiments
  chapter; placing it last occurs but is the minority.

**Per-chapter conclusions: 14 of 16.** Both forms are equally common — a numbered final subsection
("1.5 Conclusions on section 1", "N.x Summary", "N.x Chapter summary") in 7 samples, and an
unnumbered line ("Conclusions on the 1st section", "Chapter 1 Conclusions") in 7. The two samples
without them go straight from the last subsection to the next chapter.

## 6. Headings — depth and length

| | Corpus | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Numbering depth | **two levels (X.Y) in 9 of 16**; a third level (X.Y.Z) in 7, and sparse where used | **a fourth level (X.Y.Z.W) occurs in 0 of 16** |
| Second-level subsections per work | 13–34, **median ≈ 21** | flat lists of 3–14 per chapter are normal |
| Words per second-level title | **median 5**, 90th percentile 8, maximum 13 | |
| Chapter titles | 1–8 words — "Methodology", "Results", "State of the Art", "Dataset and Preprocessing", "Experimental Results" | |

Titles are plain noun phrases. **Colons, em-dashes, "vs.", and parenthetical codes do not appear in
headings anywhere in the corpus** — no sample numbers its experiments in the heading or carries an
internal identifier (hypothesis code, configuration letter, governance reference) into the contents.
Whatever internal scheme a work uses stays in its prose.

## 7. Prose — how long a paragraph and a sentence run

Measured over the body text of all 16 (7,045 paragraphs, 17,085 sentences):

| | min | median | 90th pct | 99th pct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Words per paragraph** | — | **36** | 93 | 173 |
| **Words per sentence** | — | **18** | 32 | — |
| **Sentences per paragraph** | 1 | **2** | 4 | — |

Per-sample medians run 24–72 words per paragraph and 14–25 words per sentence, so the shape is
consistent across languages and supervisors: a paragraph here is **two to three sentences making one
point**, and it fits comfortably on a third of a page. Paragraphs above ~170 words occur in every
sample but account for roughly 1% of them, and are almost always an enumeration set as running text.

This is the level at which an over-long manuscript is actually built: a body of 90-word paragraphs
of 30-word sentences is not "the same content, written densely" — at the council's reading pace it is
a different genre, and it is what makes a 250-page volume out of a 100-page argument.

## 8. The look of a page — layout, headings, typography

- **The section sign § does not appear once in the entire corpus** — not in headings, not in
  cross-references, not in citations of standards. Cross-references, which are themselves rare
  (most samples have none at all, the highest has ~16 in a whole dissertation), are written out:
  "in Chapter 2", "in section 1.7", "в разделе 3.2". **Do not use §.**
- **No internal identifiers of any kind reach the page.** No hypothesis codes, configuration
  letters, requirement or invariant references, asset tags or governance-document names. Whatever
  scheme organises the work behind the scenes stays behind the scenes, in the author's own files.
- **No editorial residue.** No `[VERIFY: …]`, `TODO`, `TBD`, comment markers or placeholder text
  survives into the published PDF in any of the 16.
- **Bold in running text is nearly absent:** bold accounts for **0.5–1.4% of words**, and that
  figure *includes* the bold headings themselves. Emphasis is carried by sentence construction, not
  by typography.
- **Bulleted/dashed lists are sparse** — 0.1–1.9 per page, median ≈ 0.5. Enumerations of aims,
  objectives and novelty items in the Introduction are the usual place for them.
- **Citations are bare numbers in square brackets** — `[12]`, or `[12, 15, 17]`. A page locator
  (`[12, p. 5]`) is used by only 2 of 16.
- **Figure and table numbering:** per-chapter numbering ("Figure 2.3") is the majority — 72% of
  figure references and 63% of table references — with continuous numbering ("Figure 12") in the
  rest. Both are accepted; be consistent within the volume. Caption position follows GOST: figure
  caption below, table title above.
- **The contents must reproduce the headings that the body actually has.** A level listed in the
  contents but absent from the text — or a heading numbered differently in the two places — is a
  formal defect, and every sample in the corpus is internally consistent on this point.
- **The em dash — is not the corpus's dash.** Its use runs 0.0–1.9 per 1,000 words, and 13 of 16 sit
  at or below 0.7. The **en dash –** is what appears, and mostly in captions ("Figure 2.1 – Title")
  and enumerations rather than as a parenthetical aside inside a sentence. Asides set off by em
  dashes are what turn a 20-word sentence into a 35-word one.
- **Italic emphasis and quotation-mark style are not regulated in practice** — italics run 0.0–6.7%
  of words across the corpus and both straight and typographic quotes occur. Neither is a defect.

### Page layout, verified by measurement on all 16

| Feature | Corpus |
|---|---|
| Page, font, margins | A4, 14 pt, 30 / 10 / 20 / 20 mm — 16 of 16 |
| Line pitch | ≈ 16 pt, i.e. **single spacing** — 15 of 16 (one sets 12 pt) |
| Body text | **justified**, with a **first-line indent of 10–12.5 mm** — 16 of 16 |
| Numbered headings | **set at the paragraph indent, aligned left — never centred.** Measured on 750+ headings: **0 of 16 samples centre them.** This is also what GOST 7.32 requires ("sections — with a paragraph indent") |
| Bold headings | optional: 5 samples bold none at all, the rest bold 36–78% of headings. Either is accepted |
| Chapter opening | each chapter starts on a new page; some open with a short orienting paragraph before the first subsection |
| Figure captions | **centred, below the figure**, "Figure 2.1 – Title", no terminal full stop |
| Table titles | **left-aligned, above the table**, "Table 2.2 – Title" |
| Split tables | carry a continuation line — "Continuation of table 2.2" / "Продолжение таблицы 2.2" — used by **10 of 16**; a table that breaks across a page without one is a formal defect |
| Subsection length | **1–6 pages, typically 2–4** (≈ 21 subsections over ≈ 82 pages of main text) |
| Page number | centred at the foot, no full stop |

Between paragraphs there is **no extra vertical space** — the first-line indent alone marks the
break. This matters when a manuscript is converted from Markdown, where a blank line is the
paragraph separator and the indent is easily lost.

## 9. Appendices — what belongs there, and what never appears

**10 of 16 have appendices at all; 6 have none.** Appendices are optional in practice, and their
page count does not enter the declared volume.

Observed content, by number of samples using it:

| Appendix content | Samples |
|---|---|
| Program code listings (the system, the model, the pipeline) | 8 |
| Copyright certificate / patent for a utility model | 7 |
| Act of implementation (акт внедрения) | 5 |
| Supplementary tables — extended results, hyperparameters, comparison matrices | 3 |
| Screenshots of the delivered system / repository | 2 |
| Ethics committee conclusion | 1 |
| Participation certificates (conference, research internship) | 1 |
| Deployment / technical operation instructions | 1 |
| An auxiliary theoretical derivation | 1 |

**Lettering.** The one Kazakh-language sample that has appendices letters them
**А, Ә, Б, В, Г, Д, Е** — i.e. Ә is kept as the second letter, unlike the Russian sequence.

**Never observed in any of the 16 — do not put these in the appendices:**

- reprints, offprints or page images of the candidate's **own published articles**;
- **screenshots of indexing databases** (Scopus / Web of Science / journal pages) offered as proof of
  publication;
- any restatement of the **list of the candidate's publications**.

The publication record has its own two carriers, and duplicating it inside the dissertation is
outside the genre: the **"Publications" and "Approbation" paragraphs of the Introduction** (and of
the annotation), and the separate defence document
[[../18-list-of-publications/structure|"List of scientific papers"]], which is what the council
actually reads for indexing, quartile and co-authors. Certificates of a *different* kind — copyright,
patent, implementation act, conference participation — are the accepted appendix material, and are
what the 7 + 5 + 1 rows above consist of.

## 10. Practical checklist

**Volume**
1. Declare the volume as **main text excluding appendices**, and state figures/tables/sources in the
   same sentence of the Introduction and the annotation ("Structure and volume of the work").
2. Target **≈ 80–100 pages / ≈ 22,000–30,000 words of main text**; treat 130 pages as the outer
   bound, not the goal. Check the word count first — it is what the page count is made of.
3. Keep the reference list at **≈ 100–150 sources / ≈ 8–11 pages**.
4. Keep tables under ~20; push per-class and per-run detail into an appendix.

**Structure**

5. **Four chapters** is the default shape: review → methods/model → development → experiments and
   results (or three, with development and experiments merged).
6. Put comparative analysis and statistical validation **inside** the results chapter as subsections.
7. Stop the numbering at **two levels**; use a third only where a subsection genuinely splits, and
   never a fourth.
8. Keep subsection titles to **≈ 5 words**, plain noun phrases, with no colons, dashes or codes.
9. Close every chapter with a conclusions subsection, numbered or unnumbered — but consistently.
10. Keep the Introduction to **≈ 6 pages** and a chapter to **≈ 15–25**; the Conclusion is 2–3 pages.

**Prose and typography**

11. Write **two-to-three-sentence paragraphs** (≈ 35–60 words) of ≈ 18–25-word sentences.
12. Never print **§**, an internal code, an editorial marker, or bold beyond the occasional term;
    write cross-references out ("in Chapter 2"), and keep them rare.
13. Bare `[12]` citations; one numbering scheme for figures and tables throughout; the contents must
    reproduce exactly the headings the body has.
14. **Set headings at the paragraph indent, left — do not centre them**; justify the body with a
    10–12.5 mm first-line indent and no extra space between paragraphs.
15. Figure caption centred below, table title left above, and a **continuation line on every table
    that breaks across a page**.
16. Use the en dash; keep em-dash asides out of sentences.

**Front and back matter**

17. Use the **8–11 merged Introduction rubrics** of section 4; do not head a "hypothesis" or "empirical
    basis" item, and merge theoretical+practical significance and approbation+publications.
18. Keep the publication record out of the appendices entirely (section 9).
19. Omit "Normative references" unless the work genuinely cites standards.
