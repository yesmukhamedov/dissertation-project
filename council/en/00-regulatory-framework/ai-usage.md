# Use of artificial intelligence technologies in preparing the dissertation

> A consolidated norm from three sources. The GD-01 Guide sets only the **check procedure**;
> the substantive requirements live in the **Rules for Awarding Degrees No. 127** (rev. 23.06.2026)
> and the **Model Statute on the Dissertation Council No. 126** (rev. 23.06.2026).
>
> ⚠ Key norm: **silence in the dissertation counts as a declaration that AI was not used**
> (para. 5-2 of the Rules). No AI section → the author is deemed to have confirmed that no AI
> was used. This is not a "safe default" — it is an affirmative statement.

## 1. Where each rule lives

| Norm | What it sets |
|------|--------------|
| **para. 5**, Rules No. 127 (as amended by MSHE order No. 4 of 06.01.2025) | the principles of independence and academic honesty; what exactly counts as a violation |
| **para. 5-2**, Rules No. 127 (added by the same order No. 4 of 06.01.2025) | entirely about AI: conditions of use, prior approval, required disclosure |
| **para. 6-1**, Rules No. 127 | the required elements of an AI citation, including a **description of the prompts** |
| **para. 7 subpara. 4)**, Rules No. 127 | AI technologies used **must be referenced** — on a par with cited sources and IP certificates |
| **para. 26**, Model Statute No. 126 | NCSTE checks for plagiarism **and for uncited AI use** |
| **para. 29**, Model Statute No. 126 | when the council commission is triggered and its deadline |
| **Ethics committee conclusion** (Model Statute, paras. 19 and 24) | must record the **absence or presence of facts** of AI use — **on the basis of the candidate's own statement** |
| **GD-01 Guide** | the procedural deadlines — [[candidate-guide]], [[../01-defense-process/application-to-council]] |
| university rules | the university **independently** approves its own rules on AI use (para. 5-2, last paragraph). At IITU this is most likely **MM-07** — we do not have the file |

## 2. What is permitted

- **Statistical sampling of variable data** — named explicitly in the principle of independence (para. 5).
- **Data processing and interpretation** — the principle of reliability expressly allows
  "the use of computer technologies and/or artificial intelligence technologies (where applicable)".
- Everything else — subject to the conditions in section 3.

**The boundary:** "the scientific results, propositions, recommendations and conclusions must be
**formulated by the author independently**" (para. 5). AI may take part in obtaining and processing
data, but not in formulating the scientific propositions.

## 3. Conditions of use (para. 5-2)

1. **Prior approval** — from the **domestic** scientific consultant, the **foreign** scientific
   consultant **and the members of the university ethics committee**. ⚠ "Prior" means **before**
   the use, not after the fact.
2. Compliance with the principles of para. 5 and the university's ethical rules.
3. **No disclosure** of restricted personal data without the data subject's consent, or of
   confidential or otherwise protected information without the rights holder's consent.
4. Reliable, safe and ethical use of information obtained via AI.

## 4. How the disclosure is formatted

**Citation elements** (para. 5 + para. 6-1) — all mandatory:

- name of the technology;
- producer (rights holder);
- version;
- date of access and/or use;
- **description of the prompts** used to obtain the result relied on in the work.

**In addition to the citation** (para. 5-2), the dissertation must describe:

- **at which stages** of the research and **in what way** the author applied AI;
- the **methods used to verify the reliability** of the data so obtained and/or of its processing
  and interpretation.

The citation style is set by the university itself (para. 6-1: Harvard / APA / MLA / Chicago / ACS
or another).

## 5. Outright prohibitions

- **Defeating plagiarism detection.** "The use of technical means and techniques, including
  artificial intelligence technologies, for the purpose of reducing or eliminating the possibility
  of detecting plagiarism is not permitted" (para. 5).
- **Children under 13** — AI may not be used in relation to them when conducting empirical
  research methods (para. 5).
- **"Restricted use" and "secret" classifications** — AI technologies may not be used in writing
  such works (para. 5).

## 6. The check and its consequences

| Step | Norm | Deadline |
|------|------|----------|
| The council sends the dissertation to **NCSTE** — for plagiarism **and for uncited AI use**. The title page and the list of references are not checked | para. 26 Model Statute | ≤10 working days after admission to defense |
| **Triggers** for a check by the council commission: facts stated in the **NCSTE certificate**, in the **official reviewers' reports**, or in **informal feedback** on the university web resource | para. 29 Model Statute | — |
| The council commission submits its **conclusion** | para. 29 | ≥8 working days before the defense |
| The council **admits to defense** or **withdraws the dissertation** | GD-01 Guide | ≥7 working days before the defense |
| The **NCSTE certificate** covering both checks goes into the attestation case (item 5) | Model Statute | ≤30 calendar days after the defense |
| A **revised** dissertation is sent to NCSTE again — for both checks | Model Statute | — |

**Sanction.** "Where **plagiarism** is found in the dissertation by the council or by the Committee,
a negative decision is taken **without the right to a repeat defense**" (para. 7). That wording is
formally tied to plagiarism; undisclosed AI use is a separate breach of the academic honesty
principle (para. 5), enforced through withdrawal from defense under para. 29 of the Model Statute
and through a negative ethics committee conclusion.

## 7. Practice across the sample corpus

Of the 16 defenses analyzed, **only one — Bazarbekov** (2026, Computer Engineering department,
programme 8D06102, i.e. the closest sample to ours) — has an AI row. His ethics committee
conclusion runs to **12 rows**, not 9:

| No. | Row | Bazarbekov's entry |
|-----|-----|--------------------|
| 10 | Absence or presence of facts of AI use in preparing the dissertation research | "AI technologies were used **exclusively for language and stylistic proofreading of texts**" |
| 11 | Approval number | `IRB-A1961` |
| 12 | Date of research approval | 26.02.2026 |

The other 15 (defended before 2026) have no such row — the 9-row form. **The target is 12 rows.**
Bazarbekov's wording also shows the acceptable genre: a narrow, concrete scope of use, with no
enumeration of tools.

None of the 16 volumes themselves contains an AI-disclosure section.

## 8. Our status ⚠

- Our volume mentions AI **only as a matter of state policy** (the AI Development Concept for
  2024–2029, the Presidential Address "Kazakhstan in the era of artificial intelligence", the Law
  of the RK "On Artificial Intelligence" No. 230-VIII) — that justifies the topic's relevance,
  it is **not a disclosure of tools**.
- **There is no disclosure section** → under para. 5-2 this amounts to a declaration that no AI was used.
- Our ethics conclusion template — [[../17-ethics-committee-conclusion/structure]] — has **9 rows**,
  with no AI row.
- **Prior approval** by both consultants and the ethics committee (para. 5-2) is not documented.

What to declare is the candidate's decision; recorded here is only the norm and what the document
set currently lacks.
