# ABSTRACT

**to the dissertation of Ysmukhamedov Nurmaganbet Seitkaliuly on the topic**  
**“Information system for automatic detection of retinal diseases**  
**based on deep learning using optical coherence tomography images,”**  
**submitted for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in**  
**educational program 8D06102 – Computer and Software Engineering**

**General characteristics of the work.** The dissertation investigates the integration of fundus image enhancement (preprocessing) with classification using convolutional neural networks (CNN) for automated diabetic retinopathy (DR) diagnosis. Preprocessing is considered an integral component of the diagnostic model, rather than ancillary data preparation, as it determines the feature space available to the network.

**Relevance of the research topic.** Diabetic retinopathy is a microvascular complication of diabetes and one of the leading causes of preventable vision loss among working-age adults. Early stages of the disease are asymptomatic; therefore, detection relies on scheduled cohort screening defined by systemic diagnosis, rather than patient referral. Screening is primarily a volume problem, while manual grading depends linearly on limited and geographically concentrated specialist time. Automation has been technically feasible for a decade; thus, the issue lies in the conditions under which demonstrative results were obtained. Images captured by different cameras, operators, and under varying mydriasis states vary systematically. Where screening capabilities are most limited, image acquisition conditions are least controlled. The transformation applied before the first convolution defines the network’s feature space. Consequently, a model described without specifying preprocessing is incompletely described, and comparisons between such models represent comparisons of partially unknown frameworks.

**Research objective** – development and experimental validation of a fundus image enhancement and CNN classification framework for automated five-class DR diagnosis, in which the eight-stage preprocessing pipeline is specified as an integral model component. It is also necessary to determine the impact of this specification on performance, transferability, and interpretability, in a controlled comparison with a configuration trained without it.

**Research tasks:** 1. Domain analysis – disease grading, screening requirements in resource-limited settings, sources of image quality loss – and formulation of the research problem (Chapter 1). 2. Methodology specification: pipeline, classification architectures, pretraining and fine-tuning strategy, explainability formalism, and evaluation protocol (Chapter 2). 3. Evaluation of the integrated configuration in-domain and on seven external data corpora, with statistical validation of results (Chapter 3). 4. Construction of a screening framework suitable for environments without Inference accelerators and with intermittent connectivity (Chapter 4).

**Research object** – the process of automated multi-stage diabetic retinopathy diagnosis from colour fundus photographs using convolutional neural networks.

**Research subject** – the set of methods for integrating preprocessing with CNN classification, as well as the properties of the resulting configuration: five-class diagnostic performance, transferability to unseen data corpora, attention consistency with expert annotation, and behaviour across various camera domains.

**Research methods.** The methodological foundation is controlled experimental comparison, in which all factors, except the one being varied, remain constant. The toolkit includes: optic disc and fovea localisation using a frozen detector; isotropic resize with padding, explicitly passed to the network as a mask channel; adaptive illumination correction and CLAHE with dual clipping in the LAB colour space; ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B3 models with a weighted focal loss Loss Function; Grad-CAM with attention–lesion overlap and IoU metrics; maximum mean discrepancy on penultimate-layer features; and 5-fold cross-validation with patient-level stratified sampling.

**Empirical base of the research** comprises eight public and clinical fundus data corpora, acquired using cameras from four manufacturers (Canon, Topcon, Kowa, Zeiss): EyePACS (approximately 35,126 graded images, for training and ablation) and its unlabelled subset (approximately 53,576), which does not intersect with the training sample, for in-domain pretraining; APTOS 2019 (approximately 3,662, for cross-corpus transfer); IDRiD (516, of which 81 have lesion masks) and Messidor-2 (approximately 1,748) for external clinical evaluation; DDR (approximately 13,673), ODIR-5K (approximately 5,000) and RFMiD (approximately 3,200) for device shift evaluation; and a Kazakh clinical corpus (60 images, 30 patients × 2 eyes) for the data-scarce regime. Results are reported for each corpus separately and are never aggregated.

**Scientific novelty** of the research consists of the following: 1. The main contribution is conceptual: preprocessing is formalised as a mandatory part of the model specification and placed in a controlled comparison, so the novelty lies not in the existence of the Pipeline, but in its treatment as an experimental object. 2. Five elements of engineering implementation are described in forms not previously combined: isotropic resize with centred padding; FOV Mask as the fourth input channel; illumination correction, scalable to the geometry of each image and applied only within the mask; channel statistics, computed over valid fundus pixels; and Canonical Orientation, the augmentation variance of which is derived from the uncertainty of landmark localisation. 3. The clip limit of the contrast stage is the minimum of two constraints – relative to the histogram and relative to the tile – and is applied stochastically, allowing the stage to perform both enhancement and regularisation functions. 4. Attention consistency is measured by the proportion of labelled lesion covered by model attention. 5. The postulated mechanism is measured directly: the “source – target” distance is computed on the penultimate layer for both configurations on six external corpora. 6. A defect common to metrics usually used for evaluating external robustness has been identified: each normalises the arm’s performance on external corpora relative to the in-domain performance of the same arm.

**Main research results.** 1. An eight-stage preprocessing pipeline has been designed and formalised: canonical flip, fovea rotation, field-of-view crop with isotropic resize, FOV Mask as the fourth input channel, illumination correction, stochastic CLAHE, and dataset-specific augmentation and normalisation. 2. An experimental programme has been conducted, the results of which are set out in the provisions submitted for defence. 3. A modular architecture for a DR screening framework has been designed for resource-constrained conditions, and a working demonstrator has been created.

**Main provisions submitted for defence:** 1. Fundus image preprocessing is a formalisable and experimentally verifiable component of the diagnostic model, not ancillary data preparation; this provision is methodological in nature. 2. The integrated configuration outperforms the baseline on the training corpus of 35,126 images, for both architectures, across all three components of the conjunctive criterion: weighted F1 by 6.54 and 6.55 percentage points, area under the curve by 0.032 and 0.036, and quadratic weighted kappa by 0.11; each interval excludes zero, comparisons remain valid after correction for multiplicity, and there is no interaction between the arm and the architecture. The arms also differ in initialisation, so the effect belongs to the configuration as a whole. 3. The contributions of individual stages are separable: cumulative ablation across eight levels increases weighted F1 from 0.7538 to 0.8193, monotonically and without a single inversion in any of the five folds; each of the seven transitions contributes between 0.0065 and 0.0143; the two photometric stages provide 41% of the increase; both parameters exhibit an internal optimum, confirmed on held-out data: clip factor 2.5 with a histogram threshold of 0.03 and a correction scale of 0.07 field-of-view diameters. 4. The distance to the training distribution decreases on each of the six external corpora – by 0.070–0.093 on the penultimate layer, with each interval excluding zero – while the transformation uses no statistics from the target corpus. 5. Competence transfers to corpora not involved in training: on the cross-corpus set, the generalisation coefficient is 0.898 versus 0.858, and weighted F1 is higher by 0.089; in five camera groupings, performance is higher in all cases, and variance is reduced by 2.4 and 3.1 times; on two external clinical corpora, the gap is 0.069 and 0.054 in weighted F1, which exceeds the minimal clinically important difference of 0.050. 6. Model attention overlaps more closely with expert-annotated lesions across all four annotated lesion types – by 0.099–0.129 at p ≤ 0.0148 – and the direction is preserved at each binarisation threshold; this provision is defended as attention consistency, not as pathology localisation. 7. Also submitted for defence are the coupled thermo-optical model of fundus tissue response to laser exposure as a theoretical contribution, and the modular architecture of the screening framework as a design contribution.

**Theoretical significance** lies in the formulation and measurement of the task: reformulation changes what is considered a complete description of a diagnostic model of this class and a fair comparison; five formalisations make previously implicit decisions explicit and verifiable, and a mechanism previously mentioned only as an explanation becomes measurable at the penultimate layer, making the mechanistic claim falsifiable (testable) independently of claims regarding performance.

**Practical significance.** Four aspects are practically available, each with its own limitation: a fully defined preprocessing mode, the parameter values of which must be re-established rather than inherited; a screening framework with preprocessing engine, Inference, Telemedicine "store and forward", and decision support modules with the doctor in the loop, integrable with PACS/EHR systems and the national eHealth platform – its demonstration instance is deployed and performs Inference, while the remaining parts remain design specifications, and data protection provisions represent by-design compliance rather than certified compliance; a protocol for accepting images from external sources, validated only on the clinical corpus for which it was created; and an argument for applicability to the national screening context, limited by the absence of field trials. Diagnosis and responsibility for it remain with the doctor.

**Reliability of results** is based on procedure, not on the magnitude of metrics: stratified grading split at the patient level, a pre-registered hierarchy of metrics, paired testing on identical cases, resampling, and a mixed-effects model. The acceptance criterion for each hypothesis was fixed before the experiment that tested it. Three caveats limit the apparatus as a whole: multiple comparison correction covers only the experiment within which comparisons were planned; several estimates are based on models of a single fold and underestimate overall uncertainty; and one experiment depends on a clinical corpus that is not subject to distribution. Four caveats relate to specific provisions: stage ranking operates only at the grouping level, and two parameter optima are properties of this corpus; distance reduction is preserved only in direction; neither the generalisation coefficient nor the lower bound for the camera group distinguishes arms, and on the second external clinical corpus the excess over the minimal clinically important difference is four thousandths; and the attention result is based on a single annotated corpus. Clinical validity, readiness for autonomous deployment, device certification, pathology localisation, and superiority over any specific published system are not submitted for defence.
