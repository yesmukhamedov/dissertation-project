## PART 1: SECTION TEXT

### F.1 What this document is, and what it is not

The reading rule comes before the document, because a certificate bearing a state emblem invites a
stronger reading than it will bear.

The document reproduced at the end of this appendix is a **certificate of entry of particulars into
the State Register of Rights to Objects Protected by Copyright**, issued by the National Institute
of Intellectual Property under the Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Kazakhstan. What it
records is that particulars of a computer program — its title, its author and its date of creation —
were entered into that register on the date it bears, and that the deposited materials were accepted
as describing the object so named.

Four things follow from that, and they exhaust what the document establishes.

It establishes **authorship as declared and registered**: the software complex implementing the
method of this dissertation is registered to the candidate as its sole author, with no co-authors
and not as a work made for hire.

It establishes **a date**. The object was created on 3 September 2026 as declared, and the
particulars were entered in the register on 4 September 2026. Neither date is a date of priority in
the patent sense.

It is **not a patent.** Computer programs are excluded from patentable subject matter under the
patent law of the Republic of Kazakhstan, and no examination of novelty, inventive step or
industrial applicability was performed or requested. Registration in this register is declarative:
the registering authority records what the applicant declares and does not test it.

It is **not a certification of the program.** No assessment was made of the software's correctness,
of its diagnostic performance, of its fitness for clinical use, or of its conformity with any
standard for medical-device software. The registration is silent on every such question, and this
dissertation asserts nothing on the strength of it. What the software does, and what may and may not
be inferred from it, is established in chapters 3 and 4 and in Appendix C, and nowhere else. The
distinction between a specified compliance posture and a certified one, drawn in chapter 4, is not
narrowed by this document.

### F.3 What was registered, and how it relates to the volume

The registered object is the **software complex as a whole**, not the demonstrator alone. It
comprises the experimental code base in which the pipeline, the classification models, the training
procedure and the explainability instruments are implemented, together with the inference service and
the operator client of the screening system. One certificate covers the whole of it, because the
implementation of the method and the system built around that implementation are one work.

Three correspondences to the volume should be stated so the document can be located in the argument
rather than admired beside it.

The preprocessing stack whose source is reproduced in Appendix A is drawn from the registered code
base. The two are the same code, and the appendix is the part of it that the central thesis of this
work requires to be auditable.

The inference service and operator client captured in Appendix C are the parts of the registered
complex that the demonstrator realises. Appendix C marks which modules of the architecture are built
and which remain specification; registration does not alter that division, and a module registered as
part of a code base is not thereby a module shown to work.

The eight-stage pipeline specified in chapter 2 is the method the registered complex implements. The
registration protects the expression of that method in code. It does not protect the method itself,
which is the subject of the volume and is placed in the public record by it.
