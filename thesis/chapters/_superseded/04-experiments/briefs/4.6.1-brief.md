### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Objective:** Specify how attention maps were produced and how their agreement with ground-truth lesion annotation was quantified, and fix — before any number is shown — exactly what such a measurement can and cannot support.

**Argumentative spine:**
- **Thesis:** The explainability claim in this dissertation is deliberately narrow, and the protocol is designed to make the narrow claim testable rather than to make a broad one plausible: it asks whether the evidence a model attends to overlaps the evidence a clinician annotated, on the same images, under a paired comparison in which the only thing that differs is the preprocessing arm.
- **Chain:**
  1. Open from §4.5.2: performance transfers, and its structure is clinically sensible. A screening system's acceptability, though, turns also on whether its outputs can be inspected — the question §1.3.3 and §2.5 set up and this section operationalizes.
  2. **State NC-14 first, before the method.** Attention maps show where a network's evidence is concentrated; they do not establish that the network detected, delineated, or clinically recognized a lesion. Overlap with an expert annotation is evidence of *alignment*, not of localization competence. Every claim in §4.6.2 and §4.6.3 is bounded by this, and the bound is a property of the method, not a hedge added afterwards.
  3. Generation: Grad-CAM (§2.5.1) computed on the final convolutional block of EfficientNet-B4, for both arms, on the same images, with the map upsampled to the input resolution and thresholded at τ.
  4. **Why a *paired* design.** Both arms produce a map for each of the same images, so per-image difficulty is differenced out; the comparison is within-image, which is what makes a corpus of 54 usable at all.
  5. Corpus: IDRiD, restricted to the **54 images carrying pixel-level lesion masks** for four lesion types — microaneurysms, haemorrhages, hard exudates, soft exudates. State plainly that mask-bearing images are a small subset and that per-type counts differ (haemorrhages 53, soft exudates 26) because not every image carries every lesion type.
  6. Metrics (§2.5.3): **ALO as primary** — the fraction of attention mass falling inside annotated lesion regions; **IoU as secondary** — a borrowed overlap measure whose interpretation is weaker here because attention maps are diffuse by construction and are not segmentations, so a low absolute IoU is expected and is not a failure.
  7. Statistics: paired one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank per lesion type, with bootstrap CIs on the paired difference; τ = 0.5 as the operating threshold, with a sweep over τ ∈ {0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} to establish that conclusions do not depend on it.
  8. **The floor-effect control**, stated as a design concern rather than a result: if attention falls entirely outside the annotation in both arms, the pair contributes no information, so the proportion of such images must be reported alongside the effect (§4.6.2).
  9. Inherited limitations: IDRiD single-centre, single-camera, small (SIR-2); no class-distribution statistic; the evaluation uses one cross-validation fold.
- **Hand-off:** §4.6.2 reports the quantitative comparison; §4.6.3 reports its per-image consistency and states what the present evidence does not cover.

**Required elements:** NC-14 stated *first*; generation method and layer; paired design and its rationale; corpus and mask-set with per-type counts; ALO primary / IoU secondary with the diffuseness caveat; statistical treatment and τ sweep; floor-effect control; inherited limitations.
