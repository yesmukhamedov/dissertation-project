### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Report how the four configurations behaved during fitting — where they reached their best validation weighted-F1, what train/validation loss gap they carried there, and how well-calibrated their output probabilities were — and derive from those quantities the analytical claim that the integrated arm behaves as a regularized configuration rather than merely a better-performing one.

**Argumentative spine (required):**
- **Thesis:** The integrated configuration does not reach its performance by fitting the training corpus harder; it reaches it by fitting a narrower hypothesis space. The evidence is a *joint* signature — earlier convergence, a smaller generalization gap, a **higher** training loss at comparable validation loss, and better-calibrated probabilities — no one of which would carry the reading alone.
- **Reasoning chain:**
  1. Open from §4.2.1: the design and its acceptance criterion are fixed; before the criterion is applied, the fitting behaviour that produced the end states is worth reporting in its own right, because it constrains how the end states may be interpreted.
  2. Render the convergence table inline: per-fold best epochs, train loss and validation loss at the best epoch, and the loss gap, for A/B/C/D.
  3. Read it: integrated arms peak at epochs 7–10 against 14–17 for baseline; the loss gap is ≈2.5× smaller (0.021–0.022 against 0.052–0.054).
  4. Make the non-obvious observation the analytical core: the integrated arms carry a **higher** training loss (0.126–0.131 vs 0.098–0.102) at a comparable validation loss. Fitting the training set *less* closely while generalizing no worse is the signature of reduced hypothesis-space capacity, not of a stronger fit. Attribute it mechanistically to illumination normalization plus the explicit field-of-view channel removing variability that carries no diagnostic signal — and label that attribution as an interpretation of the measured quantities, not as a separately measured fact.
  5. Note the within-arm spread of best epochs (±1–1.5 epochs) as evidence that the convergence regime is a reproducible property of the configuration rather than a seed artifact.
  6. State the measurement limitation plainly: per-epoch trajectories were not retained, so the analysis rests on per-fold best-epoch summaries; no curve is presented as a measured trajectory.
  7. Render **TAB-4.3** (ECE, Brier) inline and read it: the integrated arms are *better* calibrated on both backbones — ECE falls by roughly a factor of 1.7 and Brier by 0.011–0.012. Tie it to the operating-point evidence deferred to §4.2.3 (sensitivity rises without specificity falling), so calibration improvement is shown not to be a threshold artifact. Bound it: better calibration is not a deployment-readiness or certification claim (CFC-2.4, NC-9).
  8. Bound the whole section: epoch counts and convergence speed are properties of this hardware and this fixed schedule (DGL-2); the differences are properties of the integrated configuration, not of preprocessing alone (CFC-2.8).
- **Conclusion / hand-off:** The fitting behaviour is consistent with a regularized configuration and with better-behaved output probabilities; §4.2.3 applies EH-3 and EH-4 to the end-state diagnostic metrics.

### COUNTER-ARGUMENT & INHERITED LIMITATIONS

- **Counter-position:** earlier convergence is routinely explained as an easier optimization landscape rather than as regularization, and better calibration can be produced by nothing more than a shifted decision threshold. Pre-empt both: the higher-train-loss-at-comparable-val-loss signature is inconsistent with "merely easier to fit", and the calibration gain is accompanied by sensitivity rising *with* specificity (§4.2.3), which a threshold shift cannot produce.
- **Inherited limitations (SIR-2):** DGL-2 (hardware- and schedule-bound), DGL-1 (EyePACS-bound); the retained-summary limitation stated in element 4.
