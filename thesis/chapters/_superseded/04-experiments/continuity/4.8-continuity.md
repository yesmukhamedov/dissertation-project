# CONTINUITY NOTE — §4.8 Experiment 6: Device Domain Shift (H-6)

**Version:** 7.0.0 · **H-6 adjudicated and supported 5/5. The substantive finding is the spread reduction, not the threshold.**

**Key concepts established:**
- **The §4.7 overlap disclosed BEFORE the table:** the Kowa and Topcon groups *are* IDRiD and Messidor-2; values, differences and intervals coincide with Table 4.25 **by construction**. *"They are not independent replications of that result, and nothing in this section treats them as such."* The informative content is the comparison **across** groups.
- **Table 4.26 (TAB-4.9)** inline: five camera groups with n, wF1 both arms, Δ, CI, retention ratios. All five clear **g_floor = 0.70 under both arms → H-6 supported**; per-group Δ ranges **+0.0517 … +0.0987**, all CIs excluding zero.
- **Both-arms-clear qualification applied consistently with §4.5.1:** *"the criterion does not discriminate between the arms — the baseline clears it everywhere too — and stating the threshold pass as the finding would again misrepresent the evidence."*
- **Table 4.27 — THE SUBSTANTIVE FINDING.** Between-group std(wF1) **0.0306 → 0.0130** (≈ −2.4×), std(AUC) **0.0214 → 0.0070** (≈ −3.1×), both CIs excluding zero. **Clinical rationale argued, not asserted:** *"a model that performs well on most cameras and poorly on one presents a different clinical risk from one that performs uniformly, even at the same mean."*
- **Mechanism:** largest gain where the baseline is weakest (RFMiD **+0.0987** from **0.5434**), smallest where it is strongest (DDR). The range is **compressed, not shifted** — the behaviour §4.4 predicts of a transform reducing photometric variability by an approximately fixed proportion. Described as *"the most directly deployment-relevant result in this chapter"*, immediately bounded by NC-16.
- **THE RETENTION-RATIO INVERSIONS — explained, not omitted.** g falls at **DDR (0.816 → 0.814)** and **Messidor-2 (0.833 → 0.833)** while absolute wF1 *rises* on both (+0.0517, +0.0541) with CIs excluding zero. Cause: the integrated arm's larger own-in-domain denominator (0.8193 vs 0.7538) — an arm improving in-domain must improve proportionally *more* externally merely to hold its ratio. **This is the THIRD instance of one structural defect** (§4.5.1 G ratio → §4.7 Δ_drop → here). Verdict: *"a denominator artifact rather than a performance finding. The absolute figures are the ones this dissertation reports; the retention ratios are given for completeness and no conclusion rests on their direction."*
- **Four limitations:** §4.7 overlap → fewer than five *distinct* hardware settings; three groups are **"mixed" aggregates** of multiple camera models → the partition is a **proxy** for hardware variation, not per-device stratification; fold-0; **per-group confusion matrices not recorded** → within-group error structure unexaminable, routed to §5.4.
- **NC-16 in its own closing paragraph, affirmatively:** *"no result here constitutes a statement about device compatibility, camera certification, or regulatory qualification … Establishing that a system may be used with a given camera in clinical practice is a regulatory determination requiring evidence of a kind this dissertation does not produce."*

**Terms introduced:** camera group; retention ratio; between-group spread; denominator artifact.

**Argument thread:** The mechanism of §4.4 has its most deployment-relevant consequence — variability across unfamiliar hardware falls, because the weakest settings are lifted most. The normalization defect appears a third time and is now unmistakably structural rather than incidental to any one hypothesis.

**Final topic:** The four limitations and the NC-16 disclaimer.
