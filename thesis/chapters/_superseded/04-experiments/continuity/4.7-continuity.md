The integrated arm arrives higher *because it starts higher and holds its advantage*, **not** because it degrades less. *"**No claim of reduced degradation is made anywhere in this dissertation**, and the relative figures above are reported precisely so that such a reading is foreclosed."*
- **THE Δ_drop IDENTITY — the section's methodological contribution, derived in full:**
  `Δ_drop(int) − Δ_drop(base) = [wF1(int,in) − wF1(int,X)] − [wF1(base,in) − wF1(base,X)] = Δ_in-domain − Δ_external = 0.0655 − Δ wF1(X)`
  Verified on both corpora: IDRiD 0.0655 − 0.0689 = **−0.0034**; Messidor-2 0.0655 − 0.0541 = **+0.0114**. Consequence: the criterion is satisfiable **only when the arm beats baseline more abroad than at home**, so it penalizes in-domain success — an arm could improve on every corpus and still fail. Characterized as *"a defect of operationalization identified analytically, from the algebra of the measure rather than from inspection of any outcome"*, and identified as **the same structural problem flagged for the G ratio in §4.5.1, in a more severe form**. Δ_drop is retained as **descriptive only**; no verdict rests on it. Full treatment routed to **§5.4**.
