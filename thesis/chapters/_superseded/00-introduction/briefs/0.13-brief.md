### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Give the grounds on which a reader may trust the results, and — in the same section —
the three respects in which that trust must be qualified.

**Argumentative spine:**
- **Grounds (one paragraph, enumerated).** Corpus scale and multiplicity; patient-level stratified
  partitioning with leakage control; the fixed metric hierarchy rather than a metric chosen after the fact;
  paired testing on identical cases; interval estimation by resampling; cross-fold modelling; correction for
  multiplicity within the experiment where comparisons were planned; **criteria fixed before the experiments
  that tested them**; and placement against published systems without ranking, on the ground that the
  comparisons are not commensurable.
- **The pre-specification point made explicitly**, because it is the strongest of the grounds and the one a
  reader cannot verify by inspecting a table: a criterion written after a result is known can always be met.
- **Qualifications (one paragraph, three items).**
  1. Correction for multiplicity is **scoped to a single experiment**; no dissertation-wide error rate is claimed.
  2. Evaluations resting on one fitted fold quantify **evaluation-corpus sampling alone**, so their intervals
     understate total uncertainty in a known direction.
  3. One experiment depends on a corpus that **cannot be redistributed** (SB-2.2), so it is not externally
     reproducible; and calibration, though measured, is not a warrant of clinical reliability (SB-1.10).
- **Close.** One sentence: reliability here means the results are checkable against stated criteria on stated
  material — not that they are established beyond the conditions under which they were obtained.
