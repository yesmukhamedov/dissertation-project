### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Bring the reader from a clinical fact to a methodological gap in one continuous
movement, so that the goal stated next reads as forced by the argument rather than chosen.

**Argumentative spine:**
- **Thesis:** Automated DR screening is both clinically necessary and technically demonstrated; what is
  *not* settled is how the images reaching the classifier are prepared — and because that step defines the
  feature space, leaving it unformalised leaves the model itself partly unspecified.
- **Chain (five movements):**
  1. **The clinical fact.** DR is a leading cause of preventable vision loss in working-age adults, and its
     early stages are asymptomatic, so detection depends on scheduled imaging rather than on presentation.
     Screening is therefore a volume problem before it is a diagnostic one.
  2. **The capacity fact.** Manual grading scales with specialist time. The Kazakhstan figures from the
     candidate's prior architecture study give the shape of the constraint — cited as prior own work
     (SIR-4) and as *contextual framing*, with the projected deployment outcomes of the same source held
     firmly outside the dissertation's own claims (SB-1.6, SIR-8, CFC-2.3, NC-3).
  3. **The technical situation.** CNN-based screening is a demonstrated capability, and Gulshan et al.
     (2016) is named as the landmark that opened the era — a statement about historical position, never a
     numerical benchmark (SB-1.12). But that capability was established under acquisition conditions far
     more controlled than those of the settings where screening capacity is scarcest, and image quality and
     device of origin vary systematically there (OD-1).
  4. **The paradigm pre-introduction — one short paragraph.** P1 (end-to-end; preprocessing as ancillary
     data preparation) versus P2 (integrated; preprocessing as an integral model component that
     co-determines the feature space). High level only: the methodological-practice grounds belong to §1.4.
     Permitted phrasings only (CFC-2.9, SIR-9).
  5. **Why the question is answerable now.** Public corpora exist with device metadata, pixel-level lesion
     annotation and independent grading protocols, which makes a *controlled* contrast between the two
     paradigms possible rather than merely arguable; and the computational envelope of the deployment
     context (OD-6) is itself specifiable, so the comparison can be run under the constraints that matter.
- **Close:** state the boundary the whole dissertation observes — decision support within a
  physician-in-the-loop paradigm, not a standalone diagnostic device (SB-1.3) — so relevance is not read
  as a promise of clinical deployment.

**Required tables/figures:** none.
