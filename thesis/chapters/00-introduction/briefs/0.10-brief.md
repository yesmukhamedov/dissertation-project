### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Four items of practical availability, each carrying its bound in the same passage —
not in a disclaimer paragraph at the end, where a reader may stop before reaching it.

**Argumentative spine:**
1. **A fully specified, reproducible preprocessing regime.** The pipeline is defined stage by stage with its
   parameters, and its implementation is reproduced in the appendix, so it can be applied, audited or
   contested by others. **Bound:** its parameter values are properties of the corpora on which they were
   fixed and are not portable constants (DGL-5).
2. **A screening-system architecture for resource-limited settings**, with preprocessing, inference,
   telemedicine and physician-in-the-loop decision-support modules and defined integration points with
   clinical information systems. **Bound in the same sentence:** design specification only — no prototype
   was implemented, none was field-tested, and its data-protection framing is alignment by design rather
   than certified compliance (SB-4.1, SB-4.2, SB-4.3).
3. **An ingestion protocol for externally sourced images.** Practically useful where images arrive without
   controlled acquisition. **Bound:** validated against the specific clinical source it was built for, not
   for arbitrary sources (NC-15).
4. **Applicability to the Kazakhstan screening context.** Argued as a *fit* between a documented deployment
   context and a stated computational envelope. **Bound, unmissably:** the projected national outcomes cited
   from prior work — access, complication reduction, cost reduction — are **third-party projections**, are
   **not results of this research**, and substantiate nothing here (SIR-8, SB-1.6, CFC-2.3, NC-3).
5. **The standing frame:** decision support within a physician-in-the-loop paradigm; the clinician retains
   the diagnosis and the responsibility (SB-1.3).
