### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Say what changes in the *theory* of the problem if this work is accepted — four items,
none of them a result.

**Argumentative spine:**
1. **The conceptual reframing.** Preprocessing as a component of the model specification rather than as data
   preparation: it changes what counts as a complete description of a diagnostic model, and therefore what
   counts as a fair comparison between two of them. This is the item from which the others follow.
2. **Formalisations that make previously informal choices explicit and testable** — the dual-constraint clip
   limit as a minimum of two bounds; illumination correction whose scale is a function of image geometry
   rather than a constant; and an asymmetric attention–annotation overlap measure whose asymmetry is
   argued from what is clinically meaningful rather than adopted by convention. The CLAHE formalisation
   **extends the candidate's prior published work** (SIR-4).
3. **Making a postulated mechanism measurable.** The claim that preprocessing improves robustness by
   reducing distributional distance was, in this literature, an explanation rather than a measurement. The
   work supplies the middle term of that causal chain as a measurable quantity under a stated protocol
   condition — which is a theoretical contribution independently of what the measurement returned.
4. **A defect identified in a family of evaluation measures.** Measures that normalise external performance
   against an arm's own in-domain performance are not neutral instruments; the analysis is analytic and
   applies wherever such measures are used. Named here as significance, **not restated as a result**.
5. **The coupled thermal-optical model**, as a theoretical and computational contribution only (SB-1.5, SIR-6).

**Close.** One sentence bounding the whole: these are contributions to how the problem is posed and measured;
none of them is established by argument alone as a universal proposition (CFC-2.1).
