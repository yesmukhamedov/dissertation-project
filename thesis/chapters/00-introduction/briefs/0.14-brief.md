### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Present the eight corpora as a *tiered architecture* — each tier answering a distinct
question — with the properties that bound what may be concluded from each.

**Argumentative spine:**
- **Opening.** The corpora are organised by function, not assembled opportunistically; the tiering is what
  allows one trained configuration to be examined for several distinct properties without retraining.
- **Tier 1 — training and ablation.** The primary corpus, its approximate scale, its camera, and its
  grade imbalance (SB-2.1). Note the **disjoint unlabelled split used for in-domain pretraining**, separated
  by image and by patient identity from the evaluation corpus (SB-2.4) — the constraint that keeps the
  initialisation from being a leak.
- **Tier 2 — external public evaluation.** The independent public corpus used for zero-shot transfer.
- **Tier 3 — external clinical evaluation.** The two clinical corpora, with their cameras, and the
  pixel-level lesion annotation subset that makes the explainability measurement possible — stating that
  the annotated subset is small relative to the corpus.
- **Tier 4 — device domain.** The three multi-device corpora with their camera manufacturers, and the
  taxonomic caveat: two use multi-disease taxonomies from which the DR subset is mapped (SB-2.3).
- **Tier 5 — clinical corpus.** The small Kazakh clinical set with its composition, and **SB-2.2** in the
  same sentence: it cannot be redistributed, so results depending on it are not externally reproducible.
- **Close.** One sentence on what the heterogeneity costs and buys: differences in equipment, population and
  grading protocol are what make cross-corpus evaluation informative and simultaneously what forbid pooling
  results across corpora.

**Required tables/figures:** none — the full dataset architecture is TAB-4.1 in §4.1.1 and is not duplicated.
