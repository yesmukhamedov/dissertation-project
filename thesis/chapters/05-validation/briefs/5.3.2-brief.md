### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Section objective:** Measure what the integrated configuration costs relative to the baseline, and correct two inferences that the usual complexity proxies would license wrongly.

**Argumentative spine:**
- **Thesis:** At the network, the integrated configuration is very nearly free — its cost is borne in preprocessing, which was not measured — and the two conventional complexity proxies, parameter count and floating-point operations, both mispredict the measured behaviour of the architectures compared here.
- **Chain:**
  1. Table 5.6 — the measured cost of all four configurations under stated conditions.
  2. **The cheap-prior result:** the fourth channel adds ≈ 0.9 % of operations, ≈ 24 MiB of inference memory and latency within measurement noise, against the in-domain gain of §4.2.3. State this as the quantitative form of the central thesis — *and immediately state that the pipeline's own CPU cost was not benchmarked*, so "cheap" applies to the network, not to the system.
  3. **Correction one — operations do not predict time.** EfficientNet-B3 is ≈ 4.3× cheaper in operations and ≈ 2.2× lighter in parameters, yet ≈ 9 % faster at batch 16 and **slower at batch 1**. The trade-off must be argued from measured wall-clock time. SIR-7 forbids extending this to efficient architectures as a class; NC-6 forbids any optimality claim.
  4. **Correction two — parameters do not predict memory.** Training memory was ≈ 3.7 versus ≈ 13.7 GiB in the opposite order to parameter count; the constraint is activation size at 512² in full precision, and the batch-size limit of the protocol follows from that, not from model size. Note that the larger figure exceeded the device's physical memory and was serviced by host paging — a measurement condition, stated as such.
  5. **What the section licenses:** a cost statement bounded to the documented hardware and input size, usable by Chapter 6's design argument as a parameter, not as a demonstration of deployability.

**Required tables:** Table 5.6 — measured computational cost, four configurations.
