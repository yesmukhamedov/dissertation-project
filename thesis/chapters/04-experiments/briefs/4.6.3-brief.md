### CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Objective:** Establish that the aggregate effect is distributed rather than concentrated, and state — as absences, not as findings — the two things the present evidence does not cover.

**Argumentative spine:**
- **Thesis:** The effect holds for the typical image, not merely on average, which is what licenses a general statement about the configuration's attention; but the evidence is confined to one annotated public corpus, and the clinical half of H-5 was not evaluated at all.
- **Chain:**
  1. Open by stating *why* the per-image direction matters: a mean can conceal a modest gain on most images or a large gain on a minority alongside degradation elsewhere, and only the first licenses a general claim.
  2. Render the direction table (improved / worsened / unchanged per type). 65–76 % improved; 9–15 % worsened; remainder unchanged, largely the floor-effect pairs of §4.6.2. Improved-to-worsened ratio ≈ 4:1 to 8:1.
  3. Draw the licensing conclusion explicitly: *a property of the typical image rather than of a favourable subset*.
  4. **Do not explain the worsened images.** Four to eight per type is too few for a subgroup analysis, and a post-hoc pattern would be over-fitting to the sample. State the honest summary: consistent for ~7 in 10, reverses for ~1 in 10, reasons unestablished.
  5. **Limit 1 — cross-corpus.** All measurements are on IDRiD, the only available corpus with pixel-level annotation at the required resolution. **Performance transfer and attention transfer are distinct properties**; the classification results elsewhere in the chapter are not evidence for attention transfer. Attention-alignment claims are bounded to the IDRiD mask set. This forecloses exactly the inference the original section title would have invited.
  6. **Limit 2 — the G-3 gap, in its own paragraph.** H-5 contemplates a qualitative examination on the Kazakh clinical corpus — the corpus closest to the deployment context (§4.1.1, §6.4.2). **The overlays were not produced and no qualitative analysis is reported.** State it as an **absence, not a negative result**: nothing was measured, nothing is inferred. Consequences: H-5's support rests on one annotated public corpus; Appendix E is limited to the IDRiD overlays; closing the gap needs no retraining, only overlay generation and expert review; recorded in §5.4.
  7. Close with the bounded summary: better alignment, 4/4 types, threshold-robust, ~7 images in 10, largest relative gain on the least conspicuous lesion class — **a statement about alignment between model evidence and clinician annotation**, not about localization, clinical utility or readiness.

**Required elements:** the why-it-matters framing; direction table; licensing conclusion; refusal to interpret reversals, justified; cross-corpus limit with the transfer≠attention-transfer argument; the G-3 absence paragraph with its four consequences; bounded closing.

**Tables/figures:** per-image direction table (**FIG-4.13**), inline.
