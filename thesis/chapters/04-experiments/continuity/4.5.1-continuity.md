**Key concepts established:**
- **Zero-shot defined operationally:** no retraining, no fine-tuning, no adaptation; pipeline parameters fixed; **Stage 7 uses source-domain statistics**, so no property of the APTOS distribution entered the transform. This links the transfer protocol back to the §4.4.1 design property.
- **G motivated, not merely stated:** G = wF1(target)/wF1(source) asks what *fraction* of in-domain competence survives; normalizing is necessary because an arm starting higher would end higher without that indicating robustness.
- **Table 4.18 (TAB-4.6)** inline: C wF1 0.6465 / AUC 0.7940 / κ 0.7887 / **G 0.8577**; D wF1 **0.7354** / AUC **0.8263** / κ **0.8874** / **G 0.8976**. ΔwF1 **+0.0889** CI [+0.0681, +0.1197]; ΔAUC **+0.0323** CI [+0.0224, +0.0482]; both exclude zero.
- **VERDICT: H-4 supported**, G_D = 0.8976 ≥ 0.85, margin 0.048.
- **THE QUALIFICATION, in the same passage as the verdict:** G_C = 0.8577 **also clears**. The criterion does not discriminate the arms; the comparison does. **Fixed formulation for reuse:** *"the pipeline does not rescue a transfer that would otherwise fail; it improves a transfer that was already acceptable."*
- **The G-denominator asymmetry, and its forward link:** G is measured against each arm's own in-domain score, so the integrated arm faces the larger denominator (0.8193 vs 0.7538) and the ratio gain (+0.040) is **by construction more conservative** than the absolute gain (+0.089). A normalized measure of this kind **penalizes an arm for its in-domain strength**. Here mild; **in §4.7 the same pattern is severe enough to invert the measure's meaning** — flagged as *structural to ratio-based generalization measures*, which is the groundwork for the §5.4 methodological contribution.
- **Fold-0 limitation** stated with its precise consequence: the CIs are **per-instance bootstrap over the target corpus**, not per-fold intervals, so they quantify sampling variability in the evaluation rather than variability in the fitted model. **No checkpoint named** (rule 16).
